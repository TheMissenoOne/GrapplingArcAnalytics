# Ruleset-specific scoring — IBJJF against ADCC, over the Lamas action space

Runner: `analysis/ruleset_scoring.py` (point tables, envelope, chain, census) via
`scripts/ruleset_scoring_report.py` (read-only against prod `matches`). Event → family map:
`data/scouting/event_rulesets.json`, generated by `scripts/build_event_rulesets.py`. Tests:
`tests/test_ruleset_scoring.py`. Corpus read **2026-08-26**, 913 final bouts; re-running the
report reproduces every number below.

> **The corpus is live and it moved during this study.** A first pass read 909 bouts; a second,
> hours later, read 913 — four ADCC bouts had landed in between (150 → 154 with a sequence,
> 1604 → 1638 events). Every table here is the SECOND run, confirmed byte-identical on a third.
> Nothing in §§3, 4, 7 or 9 changed direction across the two, but the digits did, so the read
> date above is load-bearing and a reader comparing against a fresh run should expect drift.

**This is descriptive research, not a pre-registered PoC and not a production input.** Nothing
here feeds the site, the App, ELO, Rating V2 or any export (ADR-03). `ADCC_NEGATIVE_WINDOW` is
off and `export/*` does not import this module.

Privacy class **A, public competition data**. Every input is a `matches` row from published
footage; nothing reads a user graph or a session.

The question asked was "what is the chance each action SCORES, under each ruleset, and how do
ADCC and IBJJF differ per action". The honest answer has three parts, and the order matters:

1. **The census** (§2) says how much scoring information the corpus holds at all. It holds very
   little: 27 of 913 bouts carry an actual point value.
2. **The tables** (§3) say what the two rule books pay for. Over the seven symbols this corpus
   can resolve they differ on **exactly one value**, and everything the two books genuinely
   disagree about is unrepresentable.
3. **The measurements** (§§5–8) say what the event stream supports. A landing rate ships as a
   bound rather than a point estimate, the expected-points chain flattens within its horizon,
   and the one falsifiable check — does the inferred point leader win? — comes back at
   **0.568 [0.422, 0.703]** on ADCC, which is a refutation and is reported as one.

## 1. Method, fixed before the corpus was read

### 1.1 The quantity, and why it decomposes badly

    P(a scores | ruleset r) = P(a lands) · 1[points_r(a) > 0] · P(the moment is scoreable)

Three factors, three different problems.

**`points_r` is a lookup, not a probability.** It contributes a 0/1 mask and nothing else.

**`P(the moment is scoreable)` needs ADCC's clock and phase, and the corpus cannot supply it.**
Fixed at 1; see §4 for the measurement that forces it.

**`P(a lands)` is the one empirical term**, and it reads the `successful` flag, which
`lamas_chain` rule 3 sends to the ATTEMPT state when absent. So it is measured per family before
anything is compared (§5), and shipped as a bound rather than a point (§1.3).

### 1.2 The state space: the seven-symbol collapse

`lamas_chain.STATES` is twelve states, six of them an attempt/success pair. This module folds
each pair to its success symbol and carries `CDP` and `PGD` through, giving

    CDP · PGD · SWP · TKD · GPS · BTK · SUB

built from `lamas_chain.FAMILY` rather than written out, with an assertion that it covers the
state space exactly — a change to the Lamas states cannot leave this table behind.

**The collapse IS the comparability decision.** §7.2 of `docs/research/lamas_chain_divisions.md`
established that the attempt/success split tracks the annotation batch, and that the only thing
which crosses two differently-annotated corpora is the family-level reading that cannot see the
flag. That is the same verdict, reached the same way, and it is why the seven-symbol occupancy
is this document's primary cross-ruleset quantity (§8).

Nothing else about `lamas_chain` is re-implemented: the mapping table, the skip rule, the
chronology and the absorbing truncation all stay its decisions. `marks_of` re-pairs
`chain_of`'s steps with the events they were mapped from, positionally and safely — `chain_of`
appends one step per mapped event in array order, so the nth step is the nth accepted event, and
where the absorbing rule truncates, the steps simply run out and `zip` stops there.

### 1.3 The landing envelope, and why not a point estimate

Missing annotation is treated as **missing**, not as failure. The landing rate ships as a
Manski-style bound:

| bound | reading |
|---|---|
| `lo` | every unannotated appearance counted as a **failure** — `lamas_chain` rule 3's own reading, which is why every success rate in that module is described as a floor |
| `cc` | complete-case: drop the unannotated. Valid only if the annotation is missing at random, which across four ingest batches it demonstrably is not |
| `hi` | every unannotated appearance counted as a **success** |

The envelope's WIDTH is the missing rate, which turns the annotation problem from a caveat into
a reported quantity. Everything downstream inherits the bound: expected points is evaluated at
both ends and publishes an interval.

`CDP` and `PGD` have no attempt state, so they land by construction and report `1.000` with zero
width. **That is a definition, not a measurement.** Neither scores under either rule book, so
nothing downstream reads it as evidence; the guard in `_symbol_tally` exists only to stop the
upper bound running past 1.

### 1.4 Gating

Bout-cluster `coverage` (`stats_rigor`, ≥5 clusters and effective-n ≥2), identical to
`lamas_chain._cell`: the observed counts are a fact about the corpus and survive; the INTERVAL
is a claim about a population and is withheld below the cut with its `reason_code`.

**Two further units are graded and published, and no row gate can see either** (§5.2):

* **athletes** — a family can clear five bouts while being four people's game, in which case the
  row describes them and wears the rule book's name.
* **bout concentration** — the row gate counts fights, which cannot answer "how much of this is
  ONE fight". Measured: a single bout supplies **43%** of the `ibjjf` arm's events while the arm
  still shows 21 clusters and passes every gate above.

Both are reported rather than enforced. Turning them into gates would be a change to the
publication rule the whole report shares, which is not this document's call to make.

### 1.5 The contrast, pre-registered before it was run

Two arms, and which is primary was fixed in advance rather than picked from the results.

| arm | quantity | why |
|---|---|---|
| **PRIMARY** | symbol **occupancy** — the share of a family's actions that are this symbol | the one quantity the annotation batch cannot reach (§5.3 measures the single exception). Agresti-Caffo difference + Wilson arms + Fisher/chi-square, gated on bout clusters per side |
| **SECONDARY** | landing-rate **envelope** | bounds cross families where a point estimate does not — on one condition stated before the numbers: **the two envelopes must be disjoint**. Overlap is reported as no claim, whatever the point estimates look like |

No third arm. Expected points is a function of both plus the transition matrix, so contrasting
it would be contrasting these two again with a chain's worth of extra variance on top.

**Multiplicity is corrected** (`stats_rigor.benjamini_hochberg`, α = 0.05, `BH_ALPHA`). Seven
symbols means seven tests off one pair of corpora, which produces a "finding" from nothing about
a third of the time. The family is the **gated rows only**: an ungated row's p-value was never
published, and including it would inflate `m` and make the survivors look better than they are.

## 2. Census — what scoring information the corpus actually holds

Four buckets on the score axis, mutually exclusive, in `CENSUS_BUCKETS` precedence.

| bucket | meaning |
|---|---|
| `has_event_points` | at least one event carries a `points` field — the only place an actual point VALUE is stored. Read from `timeline` and `sequence` together, so the bucket does not depend on which importer ran |
| `has_declared_score` | no value stored, but `win_type = 'POINTS'` — the corpus saying a scoreboard decided this fight without recording what it said |
| `partial_tally_possible` | no stored score, but the sequence holds at least one action the family's table values. A PARTIAL tally, never a scoreline: an unannotated attempt cannot be told from a failed one |
| `unrecoverable` | nothing in the row from which a point can be derived |

`footage` is deliberately **not** a bucket. It is the other axis — the score is recoverable by
re-reading the video (`scripts/build_refinement_manifest` → `scripts/frame_pdf`) — and folding
it into the ladder would hide it for exactly the bouts that are rescuable.

| family | point table | bouts | `has_event_points` | `has_declared_score` | `partial_tally_possible` | `unrecoverable` | footage | + start |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `other` | no | 427 | 0 | 3 | 0 | 424 | 65 | 55 |
| `adcc` | **yes** | 185 | **16** | 6 | 106 | 57 | 125 | 112 |
| `unknown` | no | 123 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 123 | 16 | 5 |
| `cji` | no | 73 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 73 | 30 | 30 |
| `non_grappling` | no | 61 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 61 | 28 | 18 |
| `ibjjf` | **yes** | 44 | **11** | 2 | 4 | 27 | 18 | 13 |

**27 bouts in 913 carry a point value.** Everything else is inference or nothing.

`partial_tally_possible` is structurally empty for a family with no point table — those bouts
fall to `unrecoverable`, which is the honest reading: there is no per-action point value to
recover, not merely none recorded. CJI is the clean case. CJI 1 (2024) ran submission-only;
CJI 2 (2025) scores whole ROUNDS on judges' cards (`data/scouting/rulesets.json`,
`decision_model: round_cards`). Assigning per-action points there would be an invention, which
is why `cji` is excluded from the contrast rather than made a third arm.

## 3. The point tables, and the one value they disagree on

Keyed on the seven-symbol collapse. Values are what the rule book awards for ACHIEVING the
position, i.e. for the success half of the pair; an attempt scores nothing under either book.

| symbol | IBJJF v6 no-gi | ADCC (2026-08-13 snapshot) |
|---|---|---|
| `CDP` clinch dispute | 0 | 0 |
| `PGD` guard pull | 0 | 0 (−1 inside the negative window — **never applied**, §4) |
| `SWP` sweep | 2 | 2 |
| `TKD` takedown | 2 | 2 |
| `GPS` guard pass | 3 | 3 |
| `BTK` back-take | **4** | **3** |
| `SUB` submission | 0 (the bout ends) | 0 |

Sources: `ibjjf.com/books-videos` (v6 positional points) and
`adcombat.com/adcc-rules-regulations/`, both captured 2026-08-25, recorded in
`event_rulesets.json:points_sources`.

**One value.** `BTK`, 4 against 3. That is the entire ruleset difference this corpus can see,
and it is asserted in `tests/test_ruleset_scoring.py::test_the_two_tables_differ_on_exactly_one_symbol`
so the claim cannot go stale silently.

### 3.1 What is unrepresentable, and why that is the finding

Everything the two books genuinely disagree about at match-deciding resolution has **no symbol
in the Lamas vocabulary at all**:

| rule | why it cannot be represented |
|---|---|
| IBJJF advantages | no event type, no label vocabulary |
| knee-on-belly (IBJJF 2, ADCC 2) | no Lamas state; `lamas_chain` rule 2 skips it as a dwell position |
| mount (IBJJF 4, ADCC 2) | same |
| ADCC's 4-point takedown-that-passes | needs the landing position, which the event carries no field for |
| ADCC's negative phase (−1 guard pull, fleeing) | needs the match clock — §4 |
| CJI round cards | not a per-action model at all |

**So the ruleset can barely enter the number even in principle.** This is a property of the
action vocabulary, not of the tables, and it is the central result of this document: a
per-action ADCC-vs-IBJJF scoring contrast over the Lamas space is a contrast of one cell.

### 3.2 The snapshot assumption, stated because it is not verified

The only VERIFIED ADCC presets in `data/scouting/rulesets.json` are a 2026-08-13 snapshot, while
every ADCC bout in the corpus is 2019–2024 (measured over bouts with a sequence: 2019 n=2,
2022 n=62, 2023 n=25, 2024 n=65). The snapshot is applied on the assumption that ADCC's
durations and half-time structure were stable across that span. **The assumption is not checked
against period rule books**, which is why every ADCC row in `event_rulesets.json` carries
`ruleset_confidence: "snapshot_assumed_stable"` rather than `"verified"`.

**The IBJJF rows carry `"verified"` and should not, quite.** `build_event_rulesets.ruleset_for`
assigns `ibjjf-v6-no-gi` on the stated ground that every IBJJF-family event in the corpus is a
no-gi championship ("measured: 44/44"). Enumerating the tags says otherwise: `IBJJF Worlds 2023`
and `IBJJF 2025 Top 10` do not name a no-gi event, and `IBJJF Worlds 2023` is the tag on the
single largest bout in the whole IBJJF arm (§5.2). It changes no number here — the six positional
values this study uses are identical in the gi and no-gi v6 books — but the confidence LABEL
overstates, and a later question about advantages or time limits would inherit the overstatement.
**Open item**, listed in §12.

## 4. ADCC scoring windows — measured infeasible, and the refusal is visible in code

`analysis/scouting_rulesets.project_adcc_events` needs two things that live in different columns
and it needs both at once:

* **`stage`** picks the profile — `qualifying`, `final` or `overtime` — and those carry
  DIFFERENT window boundaries. Without it there is no window table to look an action up in.
* **a clock**: `ts_origin` says which clock `sequence[].ts` runs on. `bout_relative` is directly
  usable, `video_absolute` needs `video_start_seconds`, and NULL means nobody established which —
  `Match.ts_origin`'s own comment says a reader must treat that as "cannot locate", never as a
  default, because guessing is how AA-010 placed frames in a different fight.

Measured over the 185 `adcc`-family bouts:

| | count |
|---|---|
| with a `stage` | 22 |
| with a usable clock | 45 |
| `ts_origin` | `none` 134, `video_absolute` 51, `bout_relative` **0** |
| **with both** | **0** |

The intersection is **empty**. `window_applicable: false`,
`reason_code: no_bout_has_both_stage_and_clock`.

So `P(the moment is scoreable)` is fixed at 1 and the negative window is never applied.
`ADCC_NEGATIVE_WINDOW` exists as an off-by-default switch so the refusal is **visible rather
than silent** — flipping it is a research switch, never a publication setting, and
`points_for("PGD", "adcc", negative_window=True) == -1` is tested so the switch cannot rot.

### 5.3 The one channel by which annotation reaches the occupancy

The fold makes the symbol blind to `successful` — a takedown is a `TKD` whether or not anyone
marked it. Blind in the MAPPING, that is. `lamas_chain` rule 4 still truncates a chain at its
first `SUB`, and reaching `SUB` requires `successful is True`; so in a submission-won bout with
an unflagged finish, the tail events survive and inflate whatever they are.

| family | submission wins | truncated | **unflagged** | events after a finish | mapped |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `adcc` | 65 | 39 | 26 | 97 | 995 |
| `ibjjf` | 11 | 1 | **10** | 0 | 79 |
| `other` | 275 | 157 | 118 | 873 | 3910 |
| `cji` | 28 | 23 | 5 | 41 | 654 |
| `unknown` | 27 | 27 | 0 | 1 | 469 |

**The channel is small in both contrast arms and asymmetric in a way that helps.** In `ibjjf`,
10 of 11 submission wins are unflagged — but they cost **0 events after a finish**, because in
those bouts the finish is the last recorded event anyway. In `adcc` the channel is 97 events of
995, **9.7%**. So the occupancy primary is not perfectly annotation-blind, and the size of the
leak is 0% on one arm and 9.7% on the other, published rather than asserted away.

### 6.1 Reading these

**The headline answer, ADCC, is a set of intervals and not a set of numbers.** A takedown lands
somewhere in [0.279, 0.815] and therefore scores its 2 points somewhere in that range; a guard
pass in [0.246, 0.541]; a back-take in [0.172, 0.885]. The widths ARE the annotation gap — 0.536,
0.295 and 0.713 respectively — and a report that printed the complete-case column alone would
have claimed 0.602, 0.349 and 0.600 with a straight face.

**`SWP` is the worst case and the clearest illustration.** Complete-case says a sweep lands 85%
of the time. It says that off **27 annotated appearances out of 116**. The bound is
[0.198, 0.966], which is nearly the whole unit interval and is the truthful answer.

**`GPS` is the narrowest interval among the scoring symbols** (width 0.295, 43 of 61 annotated)
and the only one where the complete-case rate sits comfortably inside a usable band: a guard
pass lands roughly a third of the time, [0.246, 0.541].

**The `ibjjf` arm's complete-case column reads 1.000 four times.** It is not a finding, it is
9 of 9, 5 of 5 and 1 of 1 annotated appearances. `BTK` — the one symbol the rule books disagree
on — has **4 appearances across 3 bouts** and is refused by the gate. The single most
interesting cell in the study is the emptiest.

## 7. Expected points downstream — and the chain forgets

Pre-registered accumulation design, fixed before any corpus number was read:

    E_k(s) = Σ_{t=1..k} (P^t · r)_s ,    r_j = points_r(j) · P(j lands)

Undiscounted, finite horizon `k = HORIZON = 3`, on the empirical row-normalised seven-symbol
matrix. Undiscounted because a discount factor is a second unfitted constant and this module has
no held-out target to fit one against (`path_to_victory`'s γ was at least swept by PoC-E4).
Finite because the chain is not ergodic — `SUB` absorbs — so an infinite-horizon solve would be
dominated by whether a row happens to be all-zero. **The reward lands on ENTERING a symbol,
never on occupying it**, so a self-loop pays again and a dwell does not. Horizon 3 matches
`lamas_chain.PATHWAY_LENGTH`'s two-transitions-into-a-submission reading plus one: long enough
for a takedown to reach a pass and then the back, short enough not to extrapolate a first-order
chain past its evidence.

Evaluated at both ends of the landing envelope, so the output is an interval.

**It is points scored by EITHER athlete, not by her.** The matrix is cross-actor, the same
reading `lamas_chain` gives its matrix and for the same measured reason —
`docs/match_event_model.md` records 307 of 700 corpus bouts filing every event under one
athlete. So this answers "how many ruleset points does the next passage of the fight put on the
board", never "how many does SHE score". A signed version needs an actor field this corpus does
not have.

### 7.1 The result is that it flattens

Across all seven ADCC symbols, `E lo` runs **0.78 – 1.06** and `E hi` runs **2.96 – 4.05**. A
spread of 0.28 points at the lower bound, against envelope widths of up to 0.77 on the inputs.
**Three transitions is already long enough for the first-order chain to forget which action it
started from.**

That is the same shape as §8.6 of `lamas_chain_divisions.md`, where propagation flattens the
reward-risk balance to within ±0.074 in eleven of twelve states because the chain mixes faster
than it absorbs. Two independent layers, one corpus, the same behaviour — which is corroboration
that the property belongs to the chain and not to either implementation.

**So expected-points-downstream is not a usable discriminator between actions on this corpus,**
and it is reported here so nobody builds one on it. What survives is the immediate table (§3) and
the landing envelope (§6). The `ibjjf` arm's higher band (1.11–4.35 against 0.78–4.05) is not
evidence of anything: it is 79 events with four saturated landing rates feeding the reward
vector.

## 8. The ADCC-vs-IBJJF contrast

79 `ibjjf` appearances against 995 `adcc`. Arms: **24 vs 180 athletes, 9 shared** (38% of the
`ibjjf` arm). Multiplicity: 5 tests (gated rows only), Benjamini-Hochberg, α = 0.05.

### 8.1 What survives arithmetically

**Three occupancy differences survive BH**: `PGD` (+0.106, q=0.0030), `SWP` (+0.124, q=0.0063),
`TKD` (−0.122, q=0.0274). Read literally: the IBJJF corpus pulls guard and sweeps proportionally
more, and takes the fight down proportionally less.

**Two landing envelopes are disjoint**: `GPS` (IBJJF [0.625, 0.625] against ADCC [0.246, 0.541])
and `SUB` (IBJJF [0.500, 0.500] against ADCC [0.564, 0.642]). Both `ibjjf` envelopes have zero
width, i.e. every appearance was annotated — 8 of 8 and 26 of 26.

**`BTK`, the one symbol where the rule books actually differ, is refused by the gate** on the
IBJJF side (4 appearances, 3 bouts). The per-action ruleset contrast the study set out to make
has no evidence at the one place a ruleset difference exists.

### 8.2 Why none of it is a ruleset finding

Three confounds, all measured, any one of which is sufficient.

**The arms are different people, and one arm is nearly one fight.** 24 athletes against 180,
sharing 9. The `ibjjf` arm is worth 8.4 effective athletes and **4.8 effective bouts**, and a
single bout — Jessa Khan × Tammi Musumeci — is 43% of its events. `PGD` at 0.152 against 0.046
is as consistent with "Khan and Musumeci played guard in one long match" as with "IBJJF rewards
pulling guard". The corpus carries no field that separates the two.

**The arms are different divisions.** The `adcc` arm is mixed — its largest event contributors
include Gordon Ryan, Yuri Simoes and Giancarlo Bodoni — while all 24 named athletes in the
`ibjjf` arm are women's-division competitors (enumerated bout by bout, all 21). The corpus has
**no sex column, and `weight_class` is NULL on all 21 `ibjjf` bouts and on 142 of the `adcc`
ones**, so this cannot be adjusted for, only named.

**The arms have different provenance.** 15 of 21 `ibjjf` bouts come from events in
`build_refinement_manifest.ALREADY_FRAMED_EVENTS` — the frame-read batch — against 33 of 154 on
the `adcc` side. That is a different reading METHOD, and it is the obvious explanation for the
`ibjjf` arm's higher `successful` coverage (59.8% vs 53.5%) and its four saturated landing
rates. A frame sheet shows an outcome; a transcript often does not.

**Verdict: the surviving contrast is a contrast of corpora, not of rule books.** It is reported
in full because it is what the corpus says, and refused as a ruleset claim because of what the
corpus is. `q < 0.05` measures the chance the DIFFERENCE arose from sampling noise; it says
nothing about whether the label on the difference is "IBJJF" or "these 24 women, read off frame
sheets".

The one contrast that would be a ruleset claim — does a back-take get taken more often where it
is worth 4 than where it is worth 3 — is `BTK`, and it is ungated.

## 9. The falsifiable check, and it fails

`winner_agreement` is the only claim in this study that can be wrong against evidence the study
did not produce. Everything else describes the event stream; this compares a score INFERRED from
that stream against `winner_id`, a field no annotation batch touches. Submission and draw
finishes are excluded — a submission says nothing about who was ahead, and counting it as
agreement would inflate the rate with bouts the scoreboard never decided.

`strict` requires the `successful` flag (the landing lower bound); `lenient` credits every
appearance of a scoring symbol (the upper bound). They bracket what the corpus supports, exactly
as the landing envelope does.

| family | strict | lenient | ties / scoreless (strict, lenient) | skipped |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `adcc` | **25/44 = 0.568 [0.422, 0.703]** | 56/89 = 0.629 [0.525, 0.722] | 74, 29 | SUBMISSION 65, DECISION 2 |
| `ibjjf` | 12/13 = 0.923 [0.667, 0.986] | 13/15 = 0.867 [0.621, 0.963] | 20, 18 | SUBMISSION 11 |

**The ADCC result is a refutation.** A point tally inferred from the Lamas symbol stream picks
the actual winner 57% of the time under the strict reading, with an interval that comfortably
covers 0.50. **The pipeline in this document cannot reconstruct an ADCC scoreline.** Every
number in §6 should be read against that: they describe the event stream faithfully and they do
not add up to the scoreboard.

Two things make it worse rather than better. **Ties and scoreless bouts outnumber the decided
ones** — 74 against 44 under strict, i.e. in nearly two thirds of eligible ADCC bouts the
inferred tally cannot even name a leader. And **lenient beats strict** (0.629 vs 0.568), which is
backwards if the `successful` flag carried real signal: crediting every unannotated attempt as a
score should be noisier than crediting only the confirmed ones, and it is not.

**The IBJJF 0.923 is not a counter-result.** Thirteen bouts, from the arm that is 15/21
frame-read, whose landing rates are saturated at 1.000 — the strict and lenient readings barely
differ there because almost everything is annotated. It is the same 21 bouts, showing that where
annotation is complete the tally tracks the result, which is a statement about annotation and
not about ADCC.

## 10. What this does not do

* **Does not touch production.** No export, no site global, no App JSON, no ELO/Rating input.
  Nothing imports `analysis/ruleset_scoring` outside its own test and report script.
* **Does not apply ADCC's negative window** — §4, and `ADCC_NEGATIVE_WINDOW = False`.
* **Does not score CJI**, and does not pretend to: `points` is `None` there, not 0.
* **Does not reconstruct a scoreline.** §9. A partial tally is not a score.
* **Does not compare landing-rate POINT estimates across families** — §5, and
  `landing_rate_cross_family_comparable: false` travels in the report.
* **Does not read `actor_id` for the chain.** Cross-actor throughout, so §7 is points on the
  board rather than points by her. `winner_agreement` is the one place actor is read, and it is
  read for a tally that is then checked against the bout result.
* **Does not verify the ADCC snapshot against period rule books** — §3.2.

## 12. Open items this study found and did not fix

Both are in adjacent code, both are measured, and neither changes a number above.

**1. `build_refinement_manifest.family_of` disagrees with the real event → family map.** That
script carries a `ponytail:` placeholder classifier written while this module did not yet exist
on disk, with a comment asking for reconciliation once it landed. It has landed. The placeholder
maps every CJI event to `adcc` (and `Combat Jiu-Jitsu Worlds` to `ibjjf`), disagreeing on **73
bouts**, and **16 of the 108 entries in the committed `data/frame_pdf/refinamento_manifest.json`
are CJI bouts filed as ADCC**. CJI has no per-action point table at all (§2), so 15% of the
frame-read queue is rendering PDFs to recover points that the ruleset never awards. Fix is a
deletion — import `analysis.ruleset_scoring.family_of`, keep `{adcc, ibjjf}` — plus the pinned
assertion in `tests/test_build_refinement_manifest.py::test_family_of` and a manifest
regeneration. Not done here because the manifest is a committed artefact with a render in
flight.

**2. The IBJJF `ruleset_confidence: "verified"` overstates** — §3.2. `IBJJF Worlds 2023` and
`IBJJF 2025 Top 10` are assigned the no-gi preset on a "44/44 no-gi" claim the tag list does not
support. No number here moves; the label should be `snapshot_assumed_stable` or the two events
should get manual overrides.
