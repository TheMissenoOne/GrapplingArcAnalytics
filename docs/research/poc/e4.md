## Criterion (pre-registered, before any number below)

> the (γ, shaping) chosen by held-out AUC ships; if AUC's CI includes 0.5, PtV is demoted from site prose until it doesn't (that is ADR-03's rule applied to a metric).

And, for item 3 of the same plan cell:

> Memorylessness probe: first-order vs second-order transition model log-likelihood on held-out bouts (state = (prev, cur) pairs, pruned at min count 5). If second-order wins materially, PtV's kernel is the thing to revisit, not its γ.

Operationalised, fixed in the runner's docstring before the run:

1. **Grid** = γ ∈ {0.60, 0.70, 0.80, 0.90, 0.95} (the plan's "{0.6…0.95}", production γ=0.8 inside it) × shaping ∈ {on = 0.1 per term (production), off = 0.0}. γ=0.00 is reported too, LABELLED as an ablation outside the grid — it deletes the discounted look-ahead and leaves the 1-step Lamas reward−risk, which is what says whether the multi-step machinery earns its existence.
2. **Chosen** = the highest held-out AUC inside the grid, on the production (ActionFlow) kernel. **Change** = that config displaces production only if its PAIRED ΔAUC against (γ=0.8, shaping on), same rows, excludes 0 — the argmax of ten overlapping intervals is a forking path, the paired interval is the decision.
3. **Demotion** is judged on the BOUT-CLUSTERED interval. Eval rows inside one bout are consecutive events of one fight, so the row-level interval is anti-conservative; both are printed and the criterion reads the clustered one.
4. **Materially** (memorylessness) = positive per-step Δ log-likelihood with a bout-clustered bootstrap interval excluding 0, at α=0.5, contexts pruned at 5.
5. **Split** = the most recent 25% of sequences by date, train is everything before. Never random (ADR-03). Label = a landed submission by the same actor within the next k=5 events.
6. **Venue** = the corpus pass decides. The fixture is one athlete's ten days: it proves the instrument (it reproduces PoC-E8's numbers config-for-config), and it is not evidence about grappling.

## Reading

1. **Most of the grid is indistinguishable from production; one corner is not.** Of the 9 non-production configs on the production kernel, 7 have a paired interval covering 0 — no evidence they differ from γ=0.8 with shaping on — while 2 are significantly WORSE and 0 significantly better. The worse ones are all high-γ WITH shaping: γ=0.95, shaping on (-0.101, 41% of nodes clamped), γ=0.90, shaping on (-0.049, 7% of nodes clamped), against 6% clamped at production. For the worst of them the mechanism is visible and is not about grappling: `shaping + p_reward + γ·stay·continuation` saturates against PtV's ±1 clamp, and a value function with that share of its nodes pinned at 1 has little ordering left for an AUC to read. It is a real property of the shipped model — the clamp is load-bearing, and γ=0.8 is safe partly because it sits below where saturation starts eating the ranking. The smaller degradations sit at a clamp share close to production's, so saturation does not explain them; they are recorded as an observation, not a mechanism. Read the flat part honestly: across those 7 configs plus production the AUC span is 0.011, against a single config's own interval of 0.107. This sweep resolves nothing there. "γ=0.8 is right" is NOT what that shows — what it shows is that **no configuration outside the high-γ-with-shaping corner is distinguishable from production on this corpus** (γ ∈ {0.60, 0.70, 0.80, 0.90, 0.95} with shaping off, and γ ≤ 0.8 with it on), a much weaker and much more honest claim, and enough to close gap #12's first half: the constant is no longer unexamined, it is measured and found non-critical over the range where the model is not saturating.

2. **Shaping's effect is γ-dependent, and at production γ it is a wash.** Turning it off at γ=0.8 moves AUC to 0.689 (paired Δ +0.007 [-0.017, +0.031]; this one IS a clean paired contrast, since the other side is production itself). The interval covers 0: on this corpus the positional prior neither helps nor hurts the held-out ranking. Its justification stays what it always was — it makes thin-data nodes order sensibly, which is a presentation property, not a predictive one, and saying so is more accurate than the current silence. Above production γ the picture flips: with shaping on, γ=0.90 and γ=0.95 fall 0.049 and 0.101 below production while their shaping-off twins do not. Shaping is what pushes the value function into the clamp; γ alone does not.

3. **What the multi-step machinery buys, measured.** The γ=0 ablation — pure 1-step Lamas reward−risk plus shaping, no look-ahead at all — scores 0.693 against production's 0.683 (paired Δ +0.010 [-0.003, +0.024]). The discounted value model is NOT measurably better than the 1-step baseline here. That is the uncomfortable number in this report and it belongs in the open: PtV's extra machinery is justified today by what it *represents* (a horizon, a branch value, the dilemma/funnel metrics that need edge values), not by held-out finish prediction, where a one-step rate ties it.

4. **First-order memorylessness survives, and second order does not just fail to win — it loses.** On the production kernel, 294 of 897 held-out steps had a (prev, cur) context with ≥5 train observations (147 such contexts exist); over all scored steps the second-order model's mean log-likelihood is -4.6675 against first order's -4.4641, Δ -0.2034 [-0.2579, -0.1326], bout-clustered. The interval excludes 0 in FIRST order's favour. A pruning threshold of five is not enough support to estimate a distribution over a vocabulary this wide, so the extra context overfits and costs held-out likelihood. The plan asked whether second order wins materially; the answer is that it loses materially, which points the same way — PtV's first-order kernel is not the defect, and raising the order on this corpus would make prediction worse, not better. The sign is stable across α ∈ {0.1, 0.5, 1.0} (Δ -0.146, -0.203, -0.218), so this is not a smoothing artefact. The honest bound on all of it: most (prev, cur) contexts never reach five observations, so the probe only ever looks at the small, dense corner of the state space where a second order could have been estimated at all.

5. **External anchor: 1 of 3 agree with Lamas 2024** (back control → submission 0.21 vs 0.45; takedown → submission 0.15 vs 0.15; guard pass → guard pass 0.08 vs 0.30). Agreement is validation content for `the-data.html`. The disagreements have a structural explanation before they have a substantive one: Lamas codes ~10 coarse states, our label vocabulary runs to hundreds of nodes, and a finer state space mechanically DILUTES every single transition probability — mass that Lamas keeps inside "guard pass" our coding spreads over smash / pressure / knee-cut / long-step. So a lower number here is what a finer coding predicts, and the anchor cannot validate at face value without a state-space mapping nobody has written. What it CAN do is flag the one cell that lands on top of the published value (takedown→submission) and the one that is far enough away to be worth a look. Reported beside the criterion and never inside it — the accept/reject above does not move on these numbers.

6. **What a production change would be, if one were ever selected.** None was. Production stays γ=0.8 with shaping on.
