## Criterion (pre-registered, before any number below)

> the interaction kernel wins the E4 AUC comparison, or surfaces vulnerability edges (opp-response patterns) that ActionFlow provably cannot represent AND that survive a stability check. Either earns it a place as a second, explicitly-labelled graph; neither replaces ActionFlow.

Operationalised, fixed in the runner's docstring before the run:

1. **Win** = the paired bootstrap ΔAUC interval (interaction − ActionFlow, same held-out rows) excludes 0 in the interaction kernel's favour. Label: a landed submission by the same actor within the next k=5 events. Split: the most recent 25% of sequences by date, never random.
2. **Vulnerability edge** = an actor-switch edge (`you:X → opp:Y`) — ActionFlow cannot represent it, its endpoints belong to different fighters — surviving ≥ 80% of 200 bout-level bootstrap resamples.
3. **Venue** = the corpus pass decides. The fixture is one athlete's ten days: it reproduces the external claim and proves the instrument, and it is not evidence about grappling.

## Reading

1. **The external §1 numbers reproduce.** Our builder finds 23 actor-switch edge types on the committed fixture against the external's 23, and the largest PageRank rank shift is 14 against their reported 8–11 band. The claim that ActionFlow structurally cannot hold `you:X → opp:Y` is now established from our own code, which is the durable form of the finding (`05_EXTERNAL_POC_REVIEW.md`, "Where to be skeptical").

2. **The AUC limb is a null result on the corpus.** Interaction 0.685 [0.646, 0.723] vs ActionFlow 0.683 [0.643, 0.721]; paired ΔAUC +0.002 [-0.005, +0.010]. The interval covers 0, so limb 1 fails — no evidence the actor-aware kernel predicts finishes better, which is not the same claim as "it predicts worse".

3. **The vulnerability limb passes, on a floor it sets itself.** 464 of 1818 actor-switch edge types survive 200 bout-level resamples at ≥ 80% — 233 distinct patterns once the perspective mirror is folded. These are things ActionFlow cannot express at all: its edges join one fighter's own consecutive actions by construction (`transitions/build_graph.py`), so a cross-fighter succession has nowhere to live in it. Be honest about the bar, though: an edge observed across many bouts survives resampling nearly by definition. What the check rules out is the fixture-scale accident where one bout carries a whole edge — not much more.

4. **What this does NOT license.** A stable edge is a reproducible pattern, not a validated predictor — limb 2 is a *representational* claim and the criterion says so ("a second, explicitly-labelled graph"). Nothing here moves ActionFlow, PtV's production kernel, or any shipped number. The next cell is PoC-E4: the same harness with γ and shaping swept, on both kernels, where a genuinely better kernel would show up.

5. **Caveats that bound every number above.** The corpus label rides on `successful`, which is present on a minority of corpus events (absent reads as False, so positives are undercounted); the gate removes the one-sided bouts but cannot repair the actor noise inside the ones it keeps; the held-out window is right-censored at each bout's end, for both kernels equally; and the large |Δrank| values sit in the low-PageRank tail, where a few extra in-edges move a node dozens of ranks — the median shift (7) is the honest summary, not the top row. Note also that the interaction kernel splits the same events over 1.9× the nodes and buys no AUC with them: more resolution is not more signal here.
